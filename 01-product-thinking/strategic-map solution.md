# Strategic Discovery Map (Module 1)

## Responses
- **Write one feature from the map that, if an engineer asked “why is this a priority for our 12-week roadmap?”, you honestly could not give a strategic answer for.:** Dark & light mode - is a nice UX enhancement, there is no strategic reason why it must be delivered within the next 12 weeks.
- **Write one feature that looks “correct” for the app but has zero strategic weight, you have no idea if it helps the company make money or whether a single user actually asked for it.:** Custom app icons. It sounds like a nice personalization feature, but there’s no evidence users want it or that it improves hydration, engagement, retention, or revenue.
- **Compare V3 to your Step 1 baseline. What changed, and is the output objectively better, did the AI get smarter, or did your added PM knowledge (JTBD, effort/impact, retention) force it to be useful?:** V3 is much more useful for product discovery. The baseline mainly lists generic user needs, features, and potential problems, while V3 focuses on behavioral friction, effort vs. impact, monetization, retention and strategic trade-offs. The AI didn’t necessarily get smarter, we simply gave it better PM thinking to work with. Instead of just mapping out a product, it started helping us make decisions.
- **Key takeaway: how does this prove the PM’s role is to think while the AI’s role is to execute?:** AI is only as useful as the thinking that guides it. The PM’s job is to understand the problem, ask the right questions, spot trade-offs, and decide what actually matters. AI can do the heavy lifting: generating ideas, structuring them and exploring options quickly.
