## Pre-work · Hypothesis check
- **Role , who you are solving for (from M2):** Responsible Payer  - A parent or self-paying customer who is financially responsible for a service and needs to understand exactly what the installment agreement covers.
- **Goal , what this user is ultimately trying to achieve:** Understand one clear and predictable payment commitment—how much is financed, what the monthly payment is, and how that obligation may change over time.
- **Friction / moment of misery , the specific pain blocking their goal:** Clients and internal teams are currently forced to manage similar installment-plan requirements through repeated coordination, bespoke configurations, and individual implementation projects. They often have to manually explain available payment options, answer recurring questions, and manage customer expectations because the product setup is not sufficiently standardized or self-explanatory. Instead of selecting from reusable building blocks, each new client or use case can trigger another round of alignment, configuration, and technical adaptation.
- **Current workaround , the external tool or manual process they rely on (M2):** 1. Collect the treatment plan, invoice or cost estimate. 2. Compare it with the installment offer to understand what amount is actually financed. 3. Clarify who must sign/pay if payer and service recipient differ. 4. Contact the partner when amounts, reimbursements or future costs are unclear. 5. Recalculate the expected monthly commitment whenever the underlying cost changes.
- **Problem Hook , your one-sentence framing of the business crisis (M1):** We must prevent increasing product fragmentation from limiting the scalability of BFS's installment-plan business by replacing repeated client-specific configurations with a standardized, configurable installment-plan framework that dynamically applies the right rules for each customer, transaction, client, and market.
- **Value Proposition , the outcome your initiative promised to deliver (M1):** For BFS clients that need flexible installment solutions across different customer groups, markets, and use cases, we will provide a standardized but configurable installment-plan framework that applies reusable rules while allowing controlled variation where needed. This creates faster onboarding, easier adaptation to market and regulatory requirements, and less dependency on bespoke implementations. It is urgent because the current project-based approach limits BFS’s ability to scale efficiently into new verticals while complexity continues to grow.

## Step 3 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** Clients and internal teams are currently forced to manage similar installment-plan requirements through repeated coordination, bespoke configurations, and individual implementation projects. They often have to manually explain available payment options, answer recurring questions, and manage customer expectations because the product setup is not sufficiently standardized or self-explanatory. Instead of selecting from reusable building blocks, each new client or use case can trigger another round of alignment, configuration, and technical adaptation.
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** 46.2K of 316K installment agreements have a manual contact attempt after creation, around 14.6%. These cases show stronger signs of payment or adjustment needs: 34.35% direct debit return rate vs. 13.00% overall, 14.99% extension rate vs. 6.76% overall, plus 6.05K extended and 6.73K shortened agreements. In orthodontics, 920 of 7.12K agreements, around 12.9%, have post-creation contact, so KFO is not more contact-heavy than the overall base. The issue appears broader than one specialty and more linked to post-setup management needs.
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Responsible Payer: a parent or self-paying customer who is financially responsible for the installment agreement. Their goal is to manage an existing payment commitment independently after setup. Their friction is that they cannot resolve payment issues, status questions, or plan changes through self-service.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Payers should be able to check, resolve, extend, shorten, or adjust installment agreements digitally instead of using calls, emails, or free-text portal messages.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** BFS reduces manual support effort and scales installment agreements through a configurable self-service model. Since KFO does not show a higher post-creation contact rate than the overall base, the opportunity is not one specialty-specific fix, but a scalable model that works across different contexts.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Reduce the post-creation manual contact rate by 10-15% relative and increase the post-creation self-service rate.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Useful flexibility and payment reliability must not worsen: legitimate extensions or shortenings must remain possible, and direct debit return rate must not increase.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** Run for 6-8 weeks across relevant amount, duration, and specialty segments; scale if post-creation manual contact decreases, digital self-service actions increase, and payment reliability stays stable.
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence , one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** Based on payers needing manual help after setup and 46.2K of 316K installment agreements showing post-creation contact, I believe that enabling Responsible Payers to check, resolve, extend, shorten, or adjust agreements through self-service will result in fewer post-creation manual contacts, as measured by a 10-15% relative reduction in post-creation manual contact rate. I will protect payment reliability and useful flexibility and will make a go/no-go decision after 6-8 weeks.
